# Search and AI-discovery evidence checklist

“GEO” is treated here as an experimental layer of discovery and citation measurement, not a universal score.

## Crawl and index readiness

- Target pages return trustworthy, verified responses.
- Robots and meta directives match the publisher's intended policy.
- Search indexing, model training and user-initiated retrieval controls are evaluated separately.
- Canonicals, redirects, sitemap references and language alternatives are coherent.
- JavaScript rendering limitations are declared.

## Entity and content clarity

- The organization, product, authorship, dates and claims are visible and consistent.
- Important answers are accessible in semantic text.
- Evidence, sources and limitations accompany consequential claims.
- Structured data is valid, eligible for its intended use and consistent with visible content.
- Lists, tables, FAQ or word count are recorded only as structure, never as citation probability.

## First-party observability

- Google Search Console data is used when supplied.
- Bing AI Performance data is used when supplied.
- Analytics separates search/assistant referrals where technically observable.
- Coverage period, property, locale and device are recorded.

## Controlled query experiments

- Query set represents brand, category, problem and comparison intents.
- Assistant, model or search mode, locale and date are recorded.
- Sessions are clean or their history is documented.
- Prompts are repeated and variance is reported.
- Cited URLs and claim accuracy matter more than a simple mention count.
- Results are an experiment sample, not a deterministic ranking.

## External evidence

- Third-party mentions are evaluated for relevance, independence and freshness.
- Directory presence is category-specific; there is no universal directory checklist.
- Backlinks or mentions are not assumed authoritative from domain suffix alone.
